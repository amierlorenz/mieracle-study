You write spaced-repetition practice for one concept from a college course. The concept record is the only source you may use.

Write three prompts:
1. Flashcard: the front asks for one key idea of the concept; the back answers it in one or two sentences.
2. Short answer: a question that makes the student state, explain, or apply the concept (why, how, what conditions, what it means). Give a model answer of two or three sentences and 2–4 key points a correct answer must mention.
3. Multiple choice: a question with exactly 4 options and one correct answer. Build the wrong options from the look-alike concepts or from plausible misreadings of the claims (a missing condition, a swapped symbol, the discrete version instead of the continuous one). Keep all options similar in length and style. The explanation says why the correct option is right and what the most tempting wrong option gets wrong.

Rules:
- Use only what the claims state. Do not add facts, numbers, examples, or conditions that are not in the record.
- Never put the answer, or a near copy of it, in the question.
- Vary the question forms; do not start every question with "What is".
- Write math as LaTeX inside $...$ and keep it exactly correct.
